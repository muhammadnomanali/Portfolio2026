Culinary Chronicles

This project explores editing as a collaborative, rhetorical practice by detailing the creation of a multicultural cookbook, emphasizing the balance between structural standardization, document usability, and the ethical preservation of diverse cultural narratives.

Project Description

This project reflects on the creation of the collaborative cookbook Culinary Chronicles, illustrating how editing functions as a complex rhetorical and organizational practice rather than a purely mechanical activity. Developed within the context of Technical and Professional Editing, the project demonstrates how editors actively participate in building the infrastructures through which texts emerge, rather than simply correcting finished documents. By designing the publication's framework, establishing editorial standards, and shaping diverse recipe submissions into a coherent whole, this project highlights the editor's role in guiding a document from its earliest conceptual stages through its final publication. Ultimately, it reveals that editing transforms individual contributions into a unified, meaningful publication.

Rhetorical and Technical Decisions

In the early stages, my group and I worked at the highest conceptual levels of edit to determine the publication's audience, format, and submission processes. Drafting the Call for Papers required us to make foundational rhetorical decisions that shaped the project's direction before any recipes were even collected. As submissions arrived, we made the technical decision to develop a comprehensive style guide to regulate formatting, typography, and recipe structure. This shared framework standardized measurement systems and visual hierarchies, allowing us to integrate highly diverse contributions coherently.

Furthermore, because recipes are procedural documents, we prioritized usability by simplifying cooking terminology and ensuring ingredient lists exactly matched the sequence of preparation steps. We balanced textual instructions with images and personal anecdotes, making the rhetorical choice to transform the cookbook into a multimodal composition that readers could navigate efficiently. However, these technical standardization efforts had to be carefully balanced against ethical considerations. While consistency was necessary for clarity, I made conscious editorial choices to preserve the specific cultural significance and authentic authorial voice embedded in each contributor's family anecdote, ensuring the stories behind the recipes were respected rather than erased.

Theoretical Connections

To understand editing as a layered, infrastructural practice, I drew upon Van Buren, Buehler, and Wallenbrock's (1976) model of the "levels of edit," which ranges from large-scale conceptual interventions to sentence-level corrections. This framework clarified how my initial work on the project's mission and Call for Papers constituted high-level structural editing. As we transitioned to standardization, Adhya's (2015) research on style guides reinforced my understanding that editors do not merely revise individual texts, but build the systemic frameworks necessary for collaborative publication.

To address the functional and procedural needs of the cookbook, Cheung's (2017) principles of plain language guided our efforts to reduce cognitive load for the reader. This was further supported by Soderston’s (1985) concept of the usability edit, which directly informed our approach to visual hierarchy and spatial design, ensuring the text was highly navigable during real-time cooking. Finally, navigating the personal family traditions embedded in the recipes required a rigorous ethical lens. Clem and Cheek (2022) argue that editing is inherently embedded within power relations, as editors have the capacity to alter how marginalized voices are represented. Guided by their social justice framework, I was able to negotiate the tension between enforcing structural consistency and preserving the authentic cultural identities of the cookbook's contributors.
