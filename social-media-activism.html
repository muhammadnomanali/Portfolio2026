Social Media Activism in Pakistan

Aristotelian Rhetoric and Multimodal Digital Practices in a censored public sphere.

Back to Research

A study exploring how social media activism in Pakistan leverages Aristotelian rhetoric and multimodal digital practices to empower marginalized groups and resist authoritarianism.

Abstract

Social media activism in Pakistan has emerged as a transformative force, empowering marginalized groups and resisting authoritarianism by leveraging Aristotelian rhetoric and multimodal digital practices. This study explores the multifaceted dimensions of digital activism, examining its evolution, strategies, and impact on civic engagement within a censored public sphere.

Key Research Areas

Aristotelian Rhetoric

Analysis of logos, ethos, and pathos in constructing digital activist narratives.

Multimodal Practices

Integration of text, images, videos, and interactive elements in digital activism.

Censorship Resistance

Adaptive strategies including VPNs, encrypted communication, and content evasion.

Platform Analysis

Critical examination of Twitter and social platforms in amplifying activist voices.

Research Contributions

  • Comprehensive analysis of digital activism strategies in authoritarian contexts
  • Framework connecting classical rhetoric to modern digital communication
  • Examination of multimodal literacy as a tool for democratic participation
  • Analysis of censorship detection and evasion methodologies
  • Implications for polarization and misinformation in democratic discourse

Significance

This research contributes to understanding digital resistance in authoritarian contexts, offering a roadmap for future research and advocacy aimed at empowering marginalized communities and fostering a more inclusive and democratic public sphere.
